# First users: prepared, unsent

Problem: rushed CSV checking lets missing garment sizes or thread colours reach production and cause operator/office interruptions.

First user: the office person at the founder's friend's Ontario embroidery shop. Owner can pilot October 9, 2026; office participation itself has not been independently confirmed. This is one usable relationship route, not independent customer validation.

Independent public starting points, inspected October 6, 2026:

| Route | Observed fit | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| [Stitch Wizard](https://www.stitchwizard.ca/contact/) | Cambridge Ontario; wholesale and retail embroidery | Shop size, CSV use and interest unknown. Public business contact only. |

| [Embbrosia](https://www.embbrosia.com/contact-us) | Mississauga embroidery business; public direct contact details | Project form not research consent; workflow and interest unknown. |

Posting/recruitment rules unreviewed; do not treat as permission to message/post. |

No one has been contacted. These businesses are candidates for learning, not customers or evidence that this exact problem recurs. Prefer a single neutral founder-controlled inquiry if later authorized; do not use project quote forms to pretend to be a buyer, bulk-send, scrape contacts or infer recruitment permission from a public page.

Unsent invitation:

“I'm a former embroidery production scheduler researching how small shops check order details before production. Could I ask your office or production coordinator for 15 minutes about the last time missing details interrupted a job? I'm interested in how you handle it today; this would be a research conversation, with no demo or sales pitch. No customer files needed.”

Past-behavior guide — no pitch, no demo, no ‘would you use my tool?’:

1. What's the hardest part of checking order details before jobs go to production?
2. Tell me about the last time that happened. What did you do, step by step?
3. Why was that difficult? Who was interrupted, and what happened next?
4. What have you tried to prevent or handle it?
5. What do you dislike about those approaches?

Optional follow-ups stay factual: frequency in a defined recent period, remembered/measured delay, existing software, correction responsibility and who authorizes purchases. Do not prompt a blank-size answer or collect private order files.

Decision signal: independent recent examples plus actual office reuse and a paid commitment support further work. Friendly approval, hypothetical purchase answers, public complaints and silence do not. If configured spreadsheets or upstream required fields already solve it comfortably, shrink or stop this offer rather than build more features.
