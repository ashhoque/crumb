# Office-cleaning contract profitability: investigation and faster test

Prepared 2026-10-06. Founder scenario is synthetic; public sources below were actually inspected. No customers, contact, payment, model, product or completed validation test.

Decision: ONE GAP. We need evidence that obtainable early job records can support a useful six-month decision beyond normal costing. Narrow provisional buyer: an independent office-cleaning owner with multiple recurring sites, actual hours by site, and a contract approaching repricing/renewal. Working problem: actual labor and extra work can consume margin before the owner changes price or scope.

## Decisive evidence and limits

- [Swept profitability reporting](https://sweptworks.com/features/janitorial-profitability-tracking-software) describes recorded hours multiplied by pay scale, contract value, budgeted labor and monthly reporting. This is an existing alternative, not proof that buyers need a separate predictor.
- [Swept plans](https://sweptworks.com/compare-plans) lists starting prices of $30/month Launch, $150/month Optimize and $225/month Scale; location-band billing with an entry band up to 15 locations. Profitability is a Scale feature. Dollar currency and starting-price commitment basis were not explicit in the retrieved text; not a verified checkout quote.
- [Pricing question, February 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/janitorial/comments/1ioeo4e/help_on_pricing/) describes firsthand cleaning experience but difficulty converting wages into a company quote. Anonymous anecdote, not prevalence or payment evidence.
- [Normal-day estimating discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/cleaningbusiness/comments/1vrninv/do_you_price_cleaning_jobs_for_the_perfect_day_or/) discusses extra hours and scope creep. The initiating account name is contractgauge, so possible commercial/research bias; commenters' identities are unverified. Do not count the thread as independent demand validation.
- [Swept free costing tool](https://sweptworks.com/features/cleaning-job-costing-software) is a public substitute/research starting point. Page inspected; calculator not tested or submitted.

Bounded research used six queries and nine open/click attempts. Swept's www pricing URL failed; an Aspire template and product page exceeded fetch limits. GSA pricing's accessible page exposed no useful labeled outcomes. No usable contract-level dataset pairing early information with six-month actual profit was located; this does not prove none exists. Public bids, wage statistics and simulations cannot substitute for actual realized job costs.

## Fastest credible test, prepared but not executed

Budget: 3-5 working days and at most 8 founder hours once authorized records arrive; existing spreadsheet/manual analysis, no paid service or implementation. Access/recruitment lead time is unknown and is additional. There is no defensible calendar completion date without records.

1. Use 10-20 office contracts old enough to have six-month results, ideally from two unrelated firms. Accept existing invoice, time and expense exports without manual backfilling. Remove employee/client identifiers; use only permissioned data. If a firm has fewer usable jobs, report the smaller count and its limits.
2. Predefine the outcome: revenue less fully burdened labor, supplies, travel, equipment, supervision, owner labor and a consistently allocated overhead share during months 4-6. Report contribution margin separately. Flag uncertain cost allocation instead of asserting net profit. Include ended/lost contracts and record why they ended; don't silently exclude failures. Six-month positive profit and profitability throughout all six months are different targets.
3. Freeze information by decision time. Compare a bid-time cost estimate with a separate day-30 forecast based on actual first-month hours/costs. The latter tests an early warning, not a pre-signing prediction. Hide months 2-6 outcomes while making judgments; no future repricing or revised estimates in inputs.
4. Compare bid arithmetic, a day-30 run-rate baseline, and a prespecified manual risk checklist (hour overruns, recurring extras, unpaid supervision). Use existing tools only. Reserve later contracts for a blind check, and keep company differences visible. If an AI method is subsequently authorized, it must beat these baselines on untouched jobs; this investigation has not done that comparison.
5. Record absolute margin error, profitable/unprofitable confusion counts, particularly false reassurance, and whether any warning arrived early enough for a feasible price/scope/staffing decision. A useful flag must add something the owner did not already know.

Provisional screening signals, not statistical proof: at least 10 reconstructable outcomes without extensive backfilling; two independently useful early warnings beyond the baseline; no concealed missing-cost assumptions. Pause prediction if labels cannot be reconstructed or simple arithmetic explains the useful warnings. Small samples cannot establish a reliable accuracy percentage; then expand across firms/time before advertising a forecast.

This replay can test data feasibility and incremental early signals. It cannot prove willingness to pay, future accuracy, customer retention or that acting on a warning improves profit. Prospective use must later freeze predictions, record owner actions and check actual six-month outcomes; intervention changes outcomes and must be logged.

## Small paid-business hypothesis

Adjacent offer: a manual three-contract margin audit identifying break-even labor hours and costs that need verification. No prediction guarantee. Buyer: owner with pricing/scope authority. Market references: free costing tool; broader Swept software listed $30-$225/month starting prices, different scope and unspecified dollar currency. Recommended range: USD 75-150 once per three-contract audit, a tentative labor/value hypothesis rather than a proven market rate. Exact first test: USD 99 once, no promotion. Before repeating at this price, confirm delivery takes at most two hours and fees cover labor. A real payment plus a documented pricing/scope decision supports the offer; compliments do not. If buyers can get equivalent value from their current tool, the offer needs revisiting. No offer has been sent.
