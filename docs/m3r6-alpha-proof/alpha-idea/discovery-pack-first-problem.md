# First problem: the latest deadline must survive the handover

Prepared 6 October 2026. Research and drafts only. Nothing built, sent, posted or purchased.

My take: start with the project manager's need to establish the current deadline and whether the reviewer has acknowledged it when a job changes hands. A generic handover platform is too broad. A Saturday-only founder should first investigate a retrospective workflow audit, rather than promise live deadline monitoring.

Working problem, still provisional: **At an email-led independent translation agency, the project manager has to reconstruct the latest agreed deadline and chase reviewer acknowledgement after a change, risking lost review time and late delivery.**

## First user and buyer

Working first user: an owner acting as project manager, or a covering PM, at an independent agency with roughly 2–5 internal coordinators and freelance translators/reviewers. The size range is a targeting hypothesis, not a finding about the entire market. Prioritize teams using email plus a shared job sheet and doing separate translation and review stages. The likely buyer is the owner/operations lead. Actual purchasing authority, volume and budget remain unknown.

Do not assume a public agency page proves it uses email-led coordination, has this pain, or wants help. The founder has no product and no agency contacts. The former colleague's statement is a secondhand clue supplied in this synthetic scenario, not a customer interview or permission to contact the colleague.

## Three ranked pains

1. **Reconciling changed deadlines at review handover or PM cover.** Closest to the founder's clue; time-sensitive and potentially consequential. A historical user account supports reconstruction effort during PM cover. Frequency, minutes lost, actual late deliveries and financial loss are unmeasured.
2. **Getting the reviewer onto the correct brief/file version.** Plausible adjacent cause of rework, but not directly established by the inspected user account. Ask about it without leading the conversation.
3. **Preserving terminology decisions and feedback.** Relevant to translation quality, but less supported here and likely to widen scope. Park until actual incidents point toward it.

This ranking is a hypothesis based on evidence strength and proximity to the clue, not fabricated market scores.

## What the research changes

- A [2020 small-agency discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/TranslationStudies/comments/it999f/) describes three internal employees plus freelancers, difficult PM cover, email coordination and shared Google Docs. This is a specific historical account of handover effort; it does not establish current prevalence or reviewer deadline failures.
- [Plunet's 2021 communication article](https://www.plunet.com/en/translation-management-news/my-favorite-feature-in-plunet-8-0-optimized-e-mail-communication/) describes assignment email templates and automated acceptance/cancellation messages. Existing systems address important parts of this workflow. This is vendor documentation of that release, not a test of today's complete product or a claim it resolves every deadline change.
- [Protemos pricing](https://protemos.com/prices.html), opened 6 October 2026, publicly lists cloud agency seats at EUR 249 per manager for three months, or EUR 899 per manager annually. Those are approximately EUR 83 or EUR 75 per manager/month respectively; the latter is the vendor's rounded equivalent. This establishes an alternative's listed price, not these agencies' spending.
- [LK Translations' freelance page](https://lktranslations.co.uk/join-the-lkteam/) describes translation plus revision and carries a positive freelancer testimonial about clear instructions and prompt handovers. It is selected vendor-hosted evidence, but a useful counterexample: handover pain is not universal.
- The [ATC directory](https://atc.org.uk/atc-member-directory/) publicly describes over 200 member companies and includes proofreading/revision filters. It establishes a research route, not a target-market estimate or permission to recruit.

Alternatives to investigate before proposing software: a dated change log and explicit acknowledgement in the existing job sheet; shared-inbox notes and ownership; configuration of an existing agency management system; a workflow audit; doing nothing when changes are rare. An AI email summary could assist reconstruction but is not proof of delivery, acknowledgement or the authoritative deadline. We have not tested any substitute.

## Decision: ONE GAP

The decision-changing fact is whether current target agencies repeatedly lose review time or delivery reliability because changed deadlines are not reconciled and acknowledged, despite their existing process. Public evidence makes this credible enough to investigate; it does not justify building.

Fastest credible future investigation: three 20-minute retrospective conversations with fitting agency PMs/owners, each reconstructing one recent changed job from anonymized information. Record recency, repeat frequency, minutes chasing, actual consequences and why the existing tool/process failed. No need to import inboxes or client documents. Interviews can establish past behavior and constraints; they cannot prove sustained use or willingness to pay.

Proposed discovery threshold, not a result: continue if at least two of three fitting agencies independently describe a similar incident within the last month, can identify a material consequence or recurring effort, and have an unsatisfactory workaround. If current systems already handle it adequately, investigate configuration/setup help. If it rarely matters, stop this wedge. Three conversations are directional, not prevalence research.

Contact remains prohibited. The pack is ready for a later founder decision; no interview has occurred or been booked. Saturday 10 October 2026 is a possible review date for these materials, not a promised customer test date.

## Working commercial hypothesis

Market reference range: Protemos's listed cloud-seat monthly equivalents are approximately EUR 75–83 per manager, with annual/quarterly commitments. A narrow service must justify itself alongside that broader alternative.

Recommended model and buyer: first explore a fixed-fee retrospective audit purchased by the owner/operations lead, rather than a subscription or weekday monitoring service. Recommended exploratory range: EUR 100–200 per agency for one bounded audit. This is our hypothesis about a small, finite engagement; it is not a market-observed service price or proven willingness to pay.

Exact future test offer: **EUR 150 once**, covering one Saturday session, up to five anonymized historical jobs, a map of where deadline changes lost ownership, and a reusable handover checklist. No promotion, free month, live monitoring or guarantees. No offer has been made.

Change this hypothesis if agencies demonstrate that existing configuration solves the issue cheaply, if safe access takes excessive time, if owners will not pay for a defined audit, or if recurring measurable effort supports an ongoing product instead. Actual payment and subsequent use of the revised process would be stronger evidence than stated enthusiasm.

## Honest limits

No measured pain rate, average financial loss, customer spend, demand, TAM, adoption or willingness to pay. Only one inspected historical user account directly describes small-agency handover difficulty. No operational experience or access advantage established for the founder. Saturdays suit retrospective work; same-day operational responsibility would require a different availability commitment. Future access to confidential project information requires narrowly agreed scope and redaction; this preparation uses only public sources.
