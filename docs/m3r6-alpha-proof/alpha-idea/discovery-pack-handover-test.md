# Paper handover test — preparation only

Working question: Can a covering project manager correctly pick up a job after reviewer availability changes, using one handover note and the same underlying records?

This tests clarity and coordination, not automatic replacement search, live monitoring, repeat use, payment or prevention of late delivery. No sessions booked, participants recruited or product built. The five-job synthetic CSV supports choosing examples; it does not establish causality.

## One testable promise

A covering PM can identify the confirmed reviewer or unresolved vacancy, current deadline, outstanding acknowledgement and next action without reconstructing the whole email thread.

## Paper handover card — blank

- Job alias:
- Availability change / source timestamp:
- Original reviewer / current confirmed reviewer (or VACANT):
- Current review deadline / time zone / authoritative source:
- Client delivery deadline / time zone / authoritative source:
- Reviewer acknowledgement: confirmed / outstanding / unknown, with source:
- Correct brief/file version or reference:
- Open issue / next action / owner / next-action due time:

Do not guess any value. Mark unknowns explicitly. A nominated reviewer is not a confirmed reviewer; a notification is not acknowledgement. Use role aliases and existing redacted records, not client documents or full inbox access.

## Future session, subject to participant permission and founder authorization

Use two historical changed-reviewer jobs with sufficient existing redacted emails/job records. A/C/E are useful candidates, but the CSV alone cannot populate the card or establish correct answers. No manual backfilling required. Skip unavailable facts and note which tasks cannot be assessed.

For each case, establish an answer key from the records: current reviewer/vacancy, review deadline, acknowledgement status and next owner/action. Where records cannot establish an answer, the correct response is unknown or an explicit clarification action.

Give a fitting PM one case with the existing records alone, and a different case with the paper card plus the same kind of underlying records. Ask: "You're taking over this job. Who is reviewing it, by when, what remains unresolved, and what would you do next?"

Observe time to a correct answer, critical errors, requests for clarification, and which source they consult. Do not explain the card while timing the task. Ask afterwards what was missing or misleading.

With a second participant, reverse which case receives the card. This reduces case/order bias; a tiny session remains directional. Do not present repeated exposure to the same case as a clean speed comparison.

## Proposed signals — not outcomes

- Keep testing if participants correctly identify all assessable critical facts and unresolved items, and the card reduces reconstruction or unnecessary clarification without concealing uncertainty.
- Revise if it misstates the current reviewer/deadline, makes unconfirmed availability appear confirmed, or takes more upkeep than the original process.
- Stop this approach if the difficulty is actually finding replacement capacity rather than transferring known information, or the existing tool already provides an equally usable handover.

No statistical or causal claim from a few sessions. A task test can show comprehension and usability; subsequent authorized use must establish upkeep, repeat use, actual time savings and delivery effects. Payment remains a separate question.

## Scope and pace

KEEP: one changed-reviewer handover, explicit unknowns, source references and clear ownership.

TEST: whether another PM can continue safely with less reconstruction.

PARK: system replacement, AI extraction, integrations, vendor matching, automatic notifications and live deadline escalation.
