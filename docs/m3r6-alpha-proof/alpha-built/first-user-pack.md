# Proofpost: first print-shop conversations

Prepared 6 October 2026. Drafts only; no contact or product changes.

## Take

The next bottleneck is customer evidence. Start with the owner or prepress/customer-service person who personally handles PDF proofs at an independent commercial printer in Greater Manchester. Murbros is the strongest public starting point because it explicitly describes emailing proofs. Ask about their recent work before showing Proofpost: the app should earn its place in the conversation.

Working problem, still a hypothesis: Print-shop staff lose time keeping track of which customer proofs are approved and which need attention before production.

ONE GAP: Does this task cause repeated, consequential trouble that their current process fails to handle? A few retrospective conversations are the fastest credible way to find out. No further build is justified by the current evidence.

The README is a synthetic project assertion: a built beta with PDF upload, approval recording and outstanding-approval tracking; zero users/interviews; intended small paid B2B business. No sending, printing, payments or customer data. These claims are not independently tested capabilities or customer evidence.

## Neutral invitation — unsent draft

Subject: A short conversation about your proofing process?

Hi,

I'm the founder of Proofpost, an early print-workflow app with no users yet. I'm trying to understand how commercial printers handle customer proofs before deciding what to do with it. Could the person who handles proofs spare 15 minutes to walk me through a recent job—what happened from sending the proof to getting approval? This would be a research conversation, with no demo or sales pitch. No client files or names needed. Completely fine if it's not of interest.

Thanks,
[Your name]

For Murbros only, add this truthful sentence: “Your website mentions that you send proofs by email, which is why I thought your experience would be relevant.” Don't reuse it for shops whose workflow is unknown.

## Five questions

Open by checking that they personally handle customer proofs. Explain that anonymised descriptions are enough; don't ask them to forward customer artwork or emails.

1. What's the hardest part of your job when handling customer proofs?
2. Tell me about the last time you encountered that. What happened, step by step?
3. Why was that difficult? What did it cost in time, delays, rework or other consequences?
4. What have you tried to deal with it? What tools or workarounds did you actually use?
5. What do you dislike about those solutions? If they work well, what makes them work?

Follow the example they choose rather than feeding them “chasing” or “wrong version.” If approvals don't feature in their hardest task, retain that counterevidence. Useful probes: how often it happened in the last month; who followed up; where the final approval was recorded; what happened to the production deadline; who selected/pays for current software. Ask for actual remembered quantities and mark estimates as estimates. Avoid “Would you use my app?” and hypothetical purchase questions.

## Decision signal

Aim for three completed conversations by 13 October 2026, subject to voluntary replies; this is a proposed learning target, not a booking. If outreach is later chosen, start with Murbros and Olympic and use the others as reserves. Silence is inconclusive about demand.

Continue to an existing-beta task session only if at least two shops independently describe a recent recurring approval-tracking problem with a concrete consequence, and at least one relevant operator opts in to a separate session. This is a directional threshold for a small experiment, not market validation. If their current email/MIS workflow works well or trouble is mostly artwork correction/preflight, park approval tracking and investigate the actual task before any change.

A later, separately agreed beta session can use a dummy PDF to observe upload, approval recording and finding an outstanding proof. Watch task completion, hesitation and duplicated work. Don't treat an internal approval record as client authorisation, or a PDF screen preview as colour/prepress approval. No real client files or production decisions in discovery. Reported pain can guide the next decision; task completion, repeat use and payment still need separate evidence.
